kidDo

What happens when computing
becomes personal for kids?

The personal computers of the past shaped a generation of makers because they were understandable, hackable, and personal. They were not just tools for consuming content but devices people could make their own. kidDo reimagines those qualities for today's children and explores what happens when personal computing becomes personal for kids again.

Truly personal

Children grow up surrounded by technology, yet very little of it actually belongs to them. Educational software may be designed for children, but it still runs on phones, tablets, and computers designed primarily for adults. kidDo is different. It is a personal computer designed from the ground up for children: their hands, their abilities, and the ways they explore their world.

Not just a classroom device, not a family computer, and not a parent's phone. A device a child can proudly carry, use, personalize, and call their own.

Built for making

What made personal computers personal was not that people carried them in their pockets, but that we could control and shape them to explore our interests and fulfill our ideas.

Unlike many modern devices, largely built around content consumption, kidDo is built around creation. Children start by drawing sprites, composing music and telling stories, then move on to designing their own worlds, building games, and gradually learning to control the device in more complex ways as their ideas grow more ambitious.

Creations you can hold

Creations matter most when they can be shared. Like the devices of the past, kidDo uses cartridges. But cartridges are much more than storage - they make digital creations tangible. A game, a story, a world, or an invention can be handed to a friend as easily as passing a book across a table.

What is tangible becomes shareable, collectible, and remixable. Every cartridge can be an invitation to build on somebody else's ideas and create something new together.

Beyond the handheld

Control does not stop at the device and exploring does not end at the edge of a screen. Cartridges can also extend kidDo with entirely new capabilities, connecting digital creations to the physical world. A cartridge might add sensors, radios, cameras, remote controls, or entirely new inventions imagined by children themselves.

The goal is not to define what kidDo can do, but to give children tools for exploring, understanding, and shaping the world around them.

{% include placeholder.html label="A cartridge with a sensor or robot attached, or a doll house with lights" %}

kidDo is both a personal computing device and a research programme exploring the role of personal computing in children's lives.

At the heart of the programme is the belief that children, whatever their interests are, should be able not only to consume technology but also create and shape it. We want to investigate whether, where and how personal ownership, creation, tangible sharing, and connections to the physical world can increase children's agency: their ability to bring their own ideas into the world through understanding and action.
