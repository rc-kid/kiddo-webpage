## [RCKid: Designing a Programming Language Through Its Editors]({{ '/assets/papers/26_splash_e.pdf' | relative_url }})

Petr Maj · *SPLASH-E 2026* · extended abstract

A small non-touch screen and a D-pad make drag-and-drop blocks and typing impractical, so instead of fitting editors to an existing language, the paper lets those constraints design the language. The result is a five-tier ladder of on-device editors (asset, iconic, visual, token and text) that are all views over one program representation and one runtime, so a game a five-year-old starts by redrawing sprites can still be extended by the same child years later in code. Editing works by selecting typed holes and choosing from menus that only offer valid constructs, with prefix notation keeping the arity of every expression visible. The paper closes with open questions about skill levels, closeness to mainstream languages, and internationalization.

## [RCKid: A personal handheld for creative STEM beyond the classroom]({{ '/assets/papers/26_dev_prototyping.pdf' | relative_url }})

Petr Maj · *pro2 Network+ Summer School, July 2026* · short paper

Introduces the device and the three principles behind it: it should be useful and polished enough that children take it home rather than leave it in the classroom, it should grow with the child along a mastery ladder from asset editing to the full SDK, and cartridges should make creations tangible, shareable and hardware-extensible. The paper places RCKid among educational hardware, maker handhelds and creative programming environments, then describes the mkIII prototype (RP2350, 2.8" display, audio codec, cartridge connector) with a cost estimate of $50 to $65 per unit for small batches, and outlines the next step: a cheaper revision for a 30+ unit pilot in a local school.
</div>
